A little about me

The person behind the strategy

For a long time, I was a ghost on social media, not because I had nothing to say. I had plenty. I just didn't think anyone wanted to hear it.

On Facebook, I kept it safe: birthdays, graduations, nothing personal, nothing vulnerable. Every time I scrolled past someone who looked perfectly polished, I'd talk myself out of showing up. "I'm not that kind of girl anyway." So I stayed quiet.

Then one day I was on TikTok, and I saw something that stopped me mid-scroll: ordinary people sharing ordinary thoughts. No filter, no performance, just ideas they believed were worth saying out loud, and people were listening. Something cracked open in me.

I had to get honest with myself. I wasn't scared of social media, I was scared of being judged, being picked apart, putting something real into the world and having it rejected. And the harder truth underneath that: the fear was really about ignorance. I didn't understand the platforms, so I feared them, and I'd dressed that fear up as opinion.

So I decided to learn. Not to go viral, not to perform a version of myself that didn't exist, but to actually understand how these platforms work, what they reward, what they punish. What I learned surprised me: social media isn't a highlight reel. It's a tool, and like any tool, it's only as good as the person who knows how to use it. That shift changed everything.

And now? I love a good strategy, and I love helping people find the words for what they already know how to do. Everything I build starts with one question: who are we actually talking to, and what would make them feel seen?

These days, the people I do my best work for are coaches, founders, and creators: brilliant at what they do, quietly stuck when it comes to showing up online. That's the gap I close.

"Your presence deserves real strategy, honest content, and someone who actually gives a damn about getting it right."

I'm Favour Egbung, a Social Media Manager and LinkedIn Growth Strategist. I help coaches, founders, and creators build a presence that works for them, not one that performs for an audience.
